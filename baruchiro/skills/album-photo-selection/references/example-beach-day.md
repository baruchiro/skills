# Worked example: six photos from one day at the beach

Fictional. Not real photos, and not a template.

| ID | Photo | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| A | Family on the beach, wide | Place and who is there |
| B | Portrait, natural expression | Representative portrait; slightly messier background |
| C | Near-identical portrait | Cleaner background; adds no new feeling, so an alternate |
| D | Splash and laughter | The action at the heart of the day |
| E | Hug in a towel | Relationship and a sense of ending |
| F | Favourite bucket in the sand | A detail, only if the bucket means something |

B and C are one moment: compare, choose B, keep C as an alternate. D and E are different moments in the same event and do different jobs.

Three ways to use the same event:
1. **Short memory in a wide book.** D large on one page, E smaller with empty space on the other. Action and ending are kept; place and portrait are lost. Choose this when the book covers a long period.
2. **Central event.** Spread one is A with B (place, people). Spread two is D with E (action, ending). F replaces another detail only if it matters. The event gets more weight, so check that this fits the rest of the book.
3. **Sequence in a spread.** If the story is an attempt, a funny fall and a success, three same-size photos in order. Three unchanged portraits in a row would read as duplicates.

The user picks. The guide shows what each choice costs.
