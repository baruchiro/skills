# Guidance

Support levels: **S** = stated by one or more sources in [sources](sources.md) (numbers in brackets). **W** = suggested workflow, this skill's synthesis. Sources are professional practice and vendor guides. Treat them as input to the user's judgment.

## Purpose and scope
- Choose by contribution, not only by quality. A slightly soft photo of a rare moment can matter more than a perfect repeat. [S: 1, 4, 7, 8]
- Start with a sentence about what to remember, and mark moments the user would regret losing, rather than a photo quota. [W, built on 1, 7]
- Keep the full archive. The album is a selection. [W]

## What a photo can contribute
Context (where and with whom), the key moment, a relationship or reaction, a meaningful detail, a transition or ending. These are possible roles, not a checklist; an event does not need one of each. [S: 1, 5, 8, 10; framing is W]

## Grouping
- Moment: near-identical versions of one action. Compare and choose a representative. [S: 4, 8; terms are W]
- Event: several moments that go together. Same day is not same event; judge by place, activity, people and continuity. [W]
- Chapter: a theme or period, for orientation. A chapter divider is optional. [W]
- Chronological order with events grouped inside is a reasonable default for a period album. Growth or comparison themes can pair photos far apart in time, with a short caption. [S: 3, 7, 10 for chronology versus theme; the default is W]

## One photo or several
- One is enough when a second photo adds no information or feeling. Keep the second as an alternate outside the album. [W, built on 4]
- Several are justified when the change can be explained: before, during and after; a different relationship in a similar pose; an intentional repeat that shows growth. [S: 8; W]
- "One scene, one photo" is a hard editing exercise to find the strongest representative. It is not a family-album rule. Keeping alternates for later pairing and layout is also recommended. Sources differ here (4 versus 2); present both. [S: 2, 4]

## Spreads and pacing
- Think in spreads, the two pages seen together, rather than one page per event or equal space per event. [S: 3; W for the framing against quotas]
- Let a key photo lead and supporting photos support. If everything is large there is no hierarchy; if everything is small, expressions are lost. [S: 3, 5, 10]
- Vary pace for a reason: a quiet photo after a busy spread. [S: 1, 5, 7]
- Link photos by content, light, gesture or contrast. Colour matching is a link, not a reason to drop an important photo. [S: 1, 5, 7]
- End with a sense of closing or continuation. Do not stage an ending that is not in the material. [S: 5, 7; W]
- Add words when the photo cannot carry the fact: who, where, when, a funny line, what an object means. [S: 3, 7]
- Coverage check: see who or what was left out only because it was photographed less. This is a check, not an equality rule. [W]

## When layout changes the selection
- Crowding that hides expressions: cut repetition or give the story more room. Do not shrink everything. [W]
- A key vertical photo cropped to fit a horizontal frame: change the layout or leave margins. [W, built on 3, 9]
- Keep faces and text away from the fold and trim area. The safe-zone size comes from the product and printer, not from this guide. [S: 3, 9]
- A limited-resolution photo may suit a small print and not a full page. Use the printer's own warning and preview. [S: 9]
- Check the whole book in the preview. A screen check helps with size and crop, not colour or paper. [S: 6, 9]
- Software may do the first layout; the user keeps content decisions: groups, order, key photos. [W]

## Not claimed
- No ideal number of photos per event, page or book.
- No printer-specific size, bleed or file rule. Ask the chosen printer.
- Editing rules from art books and portfolios do not carry over unchanged to family memory.
